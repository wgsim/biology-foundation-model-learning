## Exercise 1 — Making the scale of leakage continuous  *(notebook 01)*
Change the Butina similarity cutoff of the cluster split to {0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6} and re-evaluate on Lipophilicity (`sim_thr` in notebook 01 §3).
- At each cutoff, record the **median test→train nearest Tanimoto** and the RMSE of the linear head (ECFP4, ChemBERTa-MLM, RDKit-217), and draw a graph with the *nearest similarity* on the x-axis. At what similarity do the sign and the interval of MLM − ECFP4 change?
- Evaluation criteria: ① decide the cutoffs before seeing the results, ② a table checking the number of clusters and the balance of fold sizes, ③ confirm in numbers that, because the clusters are not transitive, pairs with a similarity higher than the cutoff remain, ④ examine with the data the hypothesis that "raising the cutoff always makes it harder".

## Exercise 2 — Is notation invariance solved by data augmentation?  *(notebook 01 §8)*
A head trained only on canonical SMILES collapsed on randomized notation (Lipophilicity MLM RMSE 0.863 → 1.448). Compare three things. (a) The current method, (b) **averaging the embeddings of K randomized notations** of the training molecules, or training together with them as augmentation, (c) **always normalizing** the input at inference.
- Evaluation criteria: ① the canonical/randomized-notation test performance of the three methods in one table (scaffold split, 3 repeats), ② the change with K (e.g., 1/5/20), ③ if (c) practically removes the problem, state its assumption (can we control the input SMILES?), ④ the cost relative to ECFP (number of embeddings).

## Exercise 4 — What if you change the gallery size and the tie policy?  *(notebook 02)*
For the test queries of each fold, change the candidate gallery to {100, 500, the whole test of that fold (≈1,479), the whole data (≈7,397)} and recompute R@1, R@10 and the median rank. Draw the small galleries at random from test, but always include the correct answer. For the "whole data" gallery, encode even the train texts with the **head trained on the same fold** and put them into the candidates. Since the train candidates are texts the model saw in training, reflect in your description that this differs from a test-only gallery *not only in the number of candidates but also in the degree of exposure* (do not merge the scores of the different models of different folds into one gallery). Also change the tie policy {against the correct answer / in favor of the correct answer / average rank}.
- Evaluation criteria: ① show the chance level together for each combination, ② whether there is a condition in which the **order of the nearest-neighbor transfer vs the CLIP head changes** between random and the Pfam group, ③ compare, **defining each**, two evaluations that handle other candidates with text identical down to the letter: (i) accept all the candidates with the same text as correct (multiple correct answers, using the highest rank among them), (ii) remove duplicate texts from the gallery (state how the correct-answer mapping was maintained), ④ which definition you recommend and why.

## Exercise 5 — If you reduce false negatives  *(notebook 02 §4)*
Implement two variants so that pairs of the same Pfam group (or text identical down to the letter) do not use each other as negatives when they are in a batch together. (a) **Mask** the off-diagonal pairs of the same group in the logits, (b) a sampler that draws **only one pair per group** in each batch. A scheme that deliberately puts the same group in one batch to collect hard negatives actually increases false negatives, so if you try it, do it only as a separate variant used together with masking.
- Evaluation criteria: ① the Pfam-group split R@1/R@10 of the default InfoNCE, (a) and (b), with intervals, ② the training loss curves and the final temperature, ③ state that the result came from the fixed setting (40 epochs), and whether it is within the range of variation of the sensitivity table (notebook 02 §7), ④ 2 possible reasons if there is no effect.

## Exercise 6 — Design document for pathology FM evaluation  *(mainly reading, running is optional)*
For UNI/CONCH/UNI2-h, **you yourself** must obtain access approval on Hugging Face (check the terms and license; an agent does not agree on your behalf). Write a design document that you can write without approval. Read de Jong et al. (2025) and organize the following. (a) a metric that measures whether the center signal overwhelms the biological signal (summarize the concept of the paper's Robustness Index in your own words), (b) a split that crosses centers (leave-center-out) and a check for slide/patient-level leakage, (c) the baselines to compare (an ImageNet-pretrained encoder, simple features such as color statistics), (d) the pre-specified criterion for what result would make you accept that "there is center robustness".
- Evaluation criteria: ① a table of how the patient, slide and center units are separated in the split, ② why the baselines are *deliberately simple*, ③ include in the conclusion a sentence that does not generalize the result of one study to pathology FMs as a whole, ④ what you cannot do because of the model license (non-commercial, etc.).

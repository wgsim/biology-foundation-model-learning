## C① — Label granularity and the zero-shot ranking (track C)
- **Setup (fixed)**: Wilk 2020 PBMC 10,400-cell subsample, 13 donors, leave-one-donor-out. All the representations are frozen, and the head is cosine kNN (k = 15, the same as module 04/02). There are 5 representations: PCA (HVG, 50), Geneformer V2 `<cls>` L-2, V2 mean L-2, V1 mean L-2, V1 random initialization. There are 3 granularities: coarse 13 classes, cellxgene `cell_type` 19 classes, fine 20 classes. The fine labels were reconstructed with the same reproduction procedure as the [track C script](track_C_head_geneformer_vs_pca.py) and passed the assert. The metric is the pooled macro-F1, and the intervals were obtained with 1,000 donor-bootstrap resamples (the same resampling indices for all comparisons).
- **Definition of the primary question**: For a pair of representations, if the signs of Δ for the coarse and fine labels are opposite and **the intervals of both exclude 0**, we regard "the ranking changes" (supported).
- **Results (pooled macro-F1 [95%])**:

| Representation | Coarse (13 classes) | cellxgene (19 classes) | Fine (20 classes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCA(HVG, 50) | 0.847 [0.797, 0.880] | 0.774 [0.734, 0.828] | 0.774 [0.736, 0.826] |
| V2 `<cls>` L-2 | 0.811 [0.750, 0.845] | 0.694 [0.642, 0.729] | 0.700 [0.646, 0.734] |
| V2 mean L-2 | 0.764 | 0.653 | 0.657 |
| V1 mean L-2 | 0.551 | 0.481 | 0.471 |
| V1 random initialization | 0.354 | 0.270 | 0.255 |
| (negative control) V2, training labels shuffled | 0.057 | 0.035 | 0.034 |

- **Verdict**: The point-estimate ranking is the same at the three granularities (the order from top to bottom of the table above) and no pair flipped sign, so **it is not supported.** However, the PCA − V2 `<cls>` gap grew in the order coarse, cellxgene, fine as +0.036 [+0.018, +0.064], +0.080 [+0.063, +0.117], +0.074 [+0.056, +0.114] (fine − coarse +0.038 [+0.026, +0.064]). The V2 `<cls>` − V1 mean gap shrank to +0.260, +0.212, +0.230 (fine − coarse −0.030 [−0.053, −0.000]).
- **Caution in reading**: The change happens all at once going from the coarse labels to cellxgene (19 classes), and hardly at all going from cellxgene (19 classes) to fine (20 classes). It is not a *gradual* effect of granularity but a difference of "13-class labels vs 19–20-class labels", and the number of classes, the class sizes and the labeling criteria change together, so they cannot be separated. Each of the 20 fine classes falls within a single coarse label (0/20 exceptions in the hierarchy check).
- **Control**: The pretraining effect (V1 − V1 random) is +0.198, +0.211, +0.215 with 13/13 donors, so it is 'supported' at all three granularities. The numbers of the coarse labels are the same as in module 04 (PCA 0.847, V2 0.811, V1 = 0.847 − 0.296 = 0.551).
- **Caution**: As in module 04/02, the PCA baseline is a transductive setting that fits HVG, scaling and PCA on all cells (favoring PCA). There are classes that are absent in some donors: 6 in the coarse labels, 10 in cellxgene and 11 in the fine labels. The metric is computed from the pooled confusion, so it is defined, but it is not a per-donor value. There are 13 donors, and one dataset and one kNN each.
- **What was changed from the template**: The unit is the donor (the same as the track C MVP). The decision rule of the primary comparison was newly defined not as "the number of donor wins" but as "a rank flip that takes the interval into account". The win-count rule was used only for the pretraining control.

## C② — The same comparison on a new single-cell dataset (track C)
- **Data (downloaded after approval)**: CELLxGENE "Cross-sectional" is the PBMC of an HIV-coinfected visceral leishmaniasis study (Commun Biol 2024, doi 10.1038/s42003-024-06225-2). 1 H5AD file, **150,017,626 B**, `https://datasets.cellxgene.cziscience.com/829fdf60-add0-4ca3-9b2f-dd86b3083885.h5ad`, sha256 `98a84e43ce2f…` (the script asserts it), placed in `.cache/singlecell/` (excluded from git). The license is as follows. The Zenodo record of the same study (10.5281/zenodo.10887674) is CC BY 4.0 (checked by API). **The license notation of the CELLxGENE file itself could not be confirmed**, and it is not redistributed. Nothing was installed.
- **Setup**: The rank value encoding and the embedding extraction of module 04/01 were carried over as they are and applied to this data, and then the track C MVP was reproduced. The notebook functions cannot be imported, so they were copied, and the allow-list unpickler was kept. The primary comparison is Geneformer V2-104M `<cls>` L-2 + a logistic head (C = 1) vs PCA (HVG, 50) + the same head, leave-one-donor-out at the donor level, with the `cell_type` of the original data as the labels. There are three decision rules: the mean of the per-donor balanced-accuracy Δ ≥ 0.02, donors with Δ > 0 ≥ 70%, and the lower bound of the donor-bootstrap interval of the pooled macro-F1 Δ > 0. The author had already seen the same comparison (V2 < PCA) on the Wilk data, and fixed the setup before seeing the result on this data.
- **Data inspection**: The original has 17,308 cells and 35,477 genes, `raw.X` is integer counts, and the gene IDs are Ensembl. There are 10 donors (8 patients and the healthy controls HEC1 and HEC2, 773–3,711 cells), and **since each donor has only one disease status, the donor and the disease status cannot be separated** (confounded; donors are nested within the disease group). A balanced subsample of 9,298 cells was made with at most 1,000 cells per donor, and then **15 cell types with fewer than 100 cells (500 cells) were left out of the evaluation** (a rule fixed in advance, from 28 types to 13, 8,798 cells). 41% of the evaluation cells are `is_primary_data = False` in CELLxGENE. According to the official Census documentation, cells that duplicate *another dataset* in the corpus are `False`, and which dataset they overlap and whether it affects the results **could not be confirmed**⚠️. For some donors (0104, 0114, 0117, 0170), all the cells fall in this category. The tokenization results are as follows. V1 has 19,512 columns linked to the vocabulary, a median length of 1,487.5, and 14% of the cells truncated at 2,048 tokens. V2 has 19,263 columns, a median of 1,490 and 0% truncated. The maximum absolute difference of the padding-invariance check (alone vs batch) is 3e-8.
- **Results (13 classes, 10 donors, logistic head)**:

| Representation | Pooled macro-F1 | Mean per-donor balanced accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| PCA(HVG, 50) | 0.813 | 0.780 |
| Geneformer V2 `<cls>` L-2 | 0.772 | 0.753 |
| Geneformer V2 mean L-2 | 0.778 | 0.755 |
| Geneformer V1 mean L-2 | 0.623 | 0.611 |
| V1 random initialization | 0.481 | 0.491 |
| (negative control) V2, training labels shuffled | 0.066 | 0.093 (uniform guessing over 13 classes 0.077) |

- **Primary comparison** V2 − PCA: pooled macro-F1 Δ **−0.041 [−0.061, −0.023]**, per-donor balanced-accuracy Δ −0.027, donors with Δ > 0 4/10, so **it is not supported.** The control, the pretraining effect (V1 − V1 random), is +0.141 [+0.124, +0.158], balanced-accuracy Δ +0.120, 10/10, so it is 'supported'.
- **Sensitivity (one at a time)**: ① If PCA is fitted per fold (HVG selected with all cells), Δ is −0.031 [−0.055, −0.011] (balanced accuracy −0.015, 3/10). ② If **PCA is fitted strictly per fold** (gene filter, HVG, scaling and PCA all on the training folds only; a post-hoc analysis added after the cross-review), PCA is 0.807 (transductive 0.813) and V2 − PCA is −0.035 [−0.055, −0.017] (−0.023, 5/10). ③ With the kNN (k = 15) head it is −0.034 [−0.049, −0.018] (−0.033, 3/10; PCA 0.737, V2 0.704). ④ With V2 mean pooling it is −0.035 [−0.056, −0.017] (−0.025, 5/10). All of these are not supported.
- **Is it the same direction as Wilk?**: It is the same. The supervised head on the zero-shot embeddings of V2 did not beat PCA (Wilk logistic Δ −0.018 [−0.031, −0.009], donors 3/13. Here −0.041, 4/10). The sizes of the gap cannot be compared, because the label sets (13 classes vs Wilk's 13 classes/fine 20 classes), the platform (5′ vs 3′) and the number of donors (10 vs 13) differ. The gap of V1 against PCA (−0.190) is much larger than that of V2, and the pretraining effect (V1 − random) being positive is also the same as in Wilk.
- **Cautions and unverified**: ① 15 of the 28 cell types were left out (because small types cannot be evaluated). The remaining 13 are large types, so this result does not apply to rare types. ② The labels are the automatic and manual annotations of the original paper and can be wrong. ③ PCA is a transductive setting fitted on the cells of all the donors, so it also sees the features of the held-out donor. In this run the difference from strict per-fold fitting was small, macro-F1 0.813 vs 0.807, and we do not go so far as to say that it generally favors PCA. ③′ The 13 evaluated cell types were defined as the types with 100 or more cells *across all donors (including the evaluation donor)*. It is a population definition fixed by the number of labels, not by features or performance, but it is true that held-out label information was used. ④ **The overlap with the Geneformer pretraining corpus could not be confirmed**⚠️. It is a 2024 study, so it is likely not in V1 (pretrained in month 6 of 2021, a corpus of about 29.9 million cells, 561 public datasets), but for V2 (about 104 million cells, non-cancer) it is unknown. The model/dataset card gives the source only as "public data such as GEO and HCA", and says the list of datasets is on the side of the Nature 2023 paper, but that list was not opened. ⑤ The logistic `max_iter` is 3,000, so the convergence condition differs from track C (300) (raising it to 3,000 in track C gave the same numbers). ⑥ It is a single run and there are 10 donors.
- **Computation**: The embeddings took 128 seconds for V1, 127 seconds for V1 random initialization and 1,314 seconds (about 22 minutes) for V2-104M, and the total was 1,641 seconds (Mac MPS). The embeddings were saved together with the input keys in `.cache/capstone_runs/h2_C_new_dataset_geneformer/`. After the strict-PCA sensitivity was added, the script was re-run with the cached embeddings (106 seconds) and the earlier numbers were reproduced as they were.

## C③ — Geneformer fine-tuning vs frozen embeddings and PCA (track C)
- **Setup (fixed)**: Wilk 2020 PBMC 10,400-cell subsample, leave-one-donor-out over 13 donors, 13 coarse labels (the same split as the track C MVP and C①). The model is **Geneformer V1-10M** (`ctheodoris/Geneformer` revision `1f7fbae4…`; the same allow-list unpickler and rank value encoding as module 04/01). The configuration is the whole BERT backbone + mean pooling (excluding padding) + Dropout 0.1 + a linear head, and since V1 has no `<cls>`, mean pooling was used instead of the official first-token pooler. **The hyperparameters were fixed and not tuned**: 2 epochs, batch 32, AdamW (backbone 5e-5, head 1e-3, weight decay 0.01), 10% warmup + linear decay, the input truncated to the top-ranked **512** genes, no validation-based selection, per-fold seed = 0 + donor index. The decision rule is the same as the track C MVP, and there are two primary comparisons. One is fine-tuning vs PCA (HVG, 50) + logistic, and the other is fine-tuning vs the V1 frozen embeddings of the same 512-token input + logistic. The author had already seen the frozen-embedding results (V1 mean L-2 0.724, PCA 0.856) and fixed the setup before seeing the fine-tuning results.
- **Why V2-104M was not done**: With the same setting it took about 17 minutes/epoch (about 9,000 cells) on this Mac (MPS), so 13-fold LODO was estimated at about 7 hours and it was excluded (a value measured once before writing the script, and the log was not kept). So the question "what if the better frozen embedding, V2 (0.838), is fine-tuned" cannot be answered by this section (it was later run on a Colab A100: [C③-V2](#c③-v2--fine-tuning-geneformer-v2-104m-track-c-colab-a100-2026-11-10) below).
- **Data inspection**: The median token length is 875, and 92% of the cells are truncated at 512 tokens (0% are truncated at 2,048). The newly computed 200 frozen embeddings of V1 at 2,048 tokens agreed with the module 04/01 cache with a maximum absolute difference of 1.19e-07, confirming that the tokenization and the vocabulary order are right.
- **Results (13 classes, 13 donors; the PCA and frozen-embedding rows use a logistic head, and the fine-tuning row uses a linear head trained together with the backbone)**:

| Representation | Pooled macro-F1 | Mean per-donor balanced accuracy (min–max) |
|---|---|---|
| PCA(HVG, 50) | 0.856 | 0.842 (0.75–0.94) |
| PCA strict per fold (filter·HVG·scaling·PCA on the training folds only) | 0.854 | 0.841 |
| V1 frozen, 2,048 tokens (module 04/01 cache) | 0.724 | 0.691 |
| V1 frozen, 512 tokens | 0.673 | 0.644 |
| **V1 fine-tuned, 512 tokens** | **0.779** | 0.767 (0.69–0.85) |

- **Primary comparisons**: ① Fine-tuning − PCA has pooled macro-F1 Δ **−0.077 [−0.092, −0.056]**, balanced-accuracy Δ −0.075, donors with Δ > 0 **1/13** (H2: 0.85 vs 0.83), so **it is not supported.** ② Fine-tuning − V1 frozen (512 tokens) is **+0.107 [+0.086, +0.150]**, +0.123, **13/13, so it is supported.** For reference, fine-tuning − V1 frozen (2,048-token cache) is +0.055 [+0.038, +0.095], +0.076, 13/13. Sensitivity: fine-tuning − strict per-fold PCA is −0.074 [−0.089, −0.054] (−0.074, 1/13), the same conclusion.
- **Controls**: The positive control (the pretraining effect) covered only the first 5 donors. The pooled macro-F1 of the fine-tuning is 0.702, that of fine-tuning in the same way from random initialization is 0.269, the mean balanced-accuracy Δ is +0.392 and 5/5 are positive (the pre-specified rule was met). The negative control is 2 folds (C1, C2) of fine-tuning with only the training labels shuffled, whose balanced accuracy is 0.123 and 0.091. The uniform-guess baseline of the balanced accuracy is the inverse of *the number of classes in that donor*, so it is 0.083 for C1 and 0.091 for C2 (0.077 for a donor with all 13 classes). Since there are only two folds and the shuffled-label model is pulled toward the training marginal distribution, this baseline is an approximation. It does not contradict there being no signal, but it is not a tight test (it is far from PCA 0.79 and fine-tuning 0.76).
- **How to read it**: Fine-tuning fills much of the gap of the V1 frozen embeddings. On the same 512 tokens it went from 0.673 to 0.779, higher even than the 0.724 of the 2,048-token frozen one. But **it does not reach PCA in this setting** (−0.077, lower in 12 of 13 donors). Truncating the input to 512 alone drops the frozen embedding from 0.724 to 0.673 (−0.051), so the possibility that the 512-token truncation lowered the ceiling of fine-tuning could not be separated in this run.
- **Cautions and unverified**: ① Only V1-10M was used, there is 1 seed, and the hyperparameters were fixed and not tuned (the README strongly recommends tuning). Tuning may improve fine-tuning, so this value is a result "in this fixed setting". ② V2-104M could not be fine-tuned in this section (it was run in C③-V2). ③ The positive and negative controls were run on only some of the folds (5 and 2). ④ The training loss fell to about 0.35 at epoch 2 (there is no validation fold, so overfitting was not measured). ⑤ The mean-pooling head differs from the official classification head. ⑥ 92% of the cells are truncated at 512 tokens. ⑦ PCA is a transductive setting (the same conclusion as the strict fit). ⑧ There are 13 donors and one dataset.
- **Reproduction and review history**: 20 folds (13 + 7 controls) were run once (5,454 seconds in total; about 216 seconds per fold when alone, and 300–420 seconds in the intervals run concurrently with other jobs). With the final script that reflects the cross-review, **the report was rebuilt from the cached fold results** (55 seconds) and the numbers were the same. What was incorporated is as follows. The "500 tokens" notation in an output label was corrected to match the code (`MAX_LEN = 512`) (the typo remains in the log of the 1st run). The resume cache was moved to a directory of the settings·data fingerprint (after confirming that the settings, data hash, revision and package versions of the 1st run matched the new fingerprint, the files were moved). A `PRIMARY` marking error was fixed. The strict per-fold PCA sensitivity was added (**post-hoc analysis**).

## C③-V2 — Fine-tuning Geneformer V2-104M (track C, Colab A100, 2026-11-10)
- **Why it is separate**: C③ ran only V1-10M. What remained was whether fine-tuning V2-104M (0.838), whose frozen embedding is better, would exceed PCA. On the Mac (MPS) it is about 8 seconds/step, so only the training was run on a Colab A100 (user approval: A100, fp32), and the baselines and the report were made on the Mac. Script: [h2_C_geneformer_v2_finetune.py](h2_C_geneformer_v2_finetune.py) (stages `check`/`bench`/`train`/`report`).
- **Setup (fixed)**: The same data, split, hyperparameters and decision rule as C③, and **the model (and what comes with it) and the pooling differ.** V2 differs not only in the weights but also in the token, median and Ensembl dictionary (gc104M) and the special tokens, so the number of genes within the 512 tokens also goes from 512 to 510. The model is Geneformer V2-104M (the same revision `1f7fbae4…`). The input is `<cls>` + the top 510 genes + `<eos>` = 512 tokens, and the configuration is the whole BERT backbone + the `<cls>` position of the last layer + Dropout 0.1 + a linear head (V1 had no `<cls>`, so it used mean pooling). 2 epochs, batch 32, AdamW (backbone 5e-5, head 1e-3, weight decay 0.01), 10% warmup + linear decay, fp32, no validation-based selection, 1 seed. There are two primary comparisons: fine-tuning vs PCA (HVG, 50) + logistic, and fine-tuning vs the V2 frozen `<cls>` + logistic of the same 512 tokens. The author fixed the setup having already seen the results of C③ (V1 fine-tuning −0.077), V2 frozen (4,096 tokens 0.838) and PCA (0.856), and fixed it before seeing the V2 fine-tuning results. What was seen before the main run was the A100 benchmark output (the step time and memory, and the training loss of the first few dozen steps of one fold), not an evaluation metric.
- **Data inspection**: The token sha256 is the same on the Mac and the VM (`4c43f5b3abea…`; the input h5ad sha256 `6ed51086…` was also compared after uploading). The **first 100 cells** of the newly computed 4,096-token V2 frozen `<cls>` on the Mac agree with the module 04/01 cache with a maximum absolute difference of 0.00 (the `check` stage). The remaining cells and the provenance of the cache file are not re-checked in the report stage. 92% of the cells are truncated at 512 tokens.
- **Results (13 classes, 13 donors; the PCA and frozen rows use a logistic head, and the fine-tuning row uses a linear head trained together with the backbone)**:

| Representation | Pooled macro-F1 | Mean per-donor balanced accuracy (min–max) |
|---|---|---|

## Common findings
1. **The template structure could be carried over this time as well.** What was changed was only the unit (from seed to assay, donor, repeat or layer) and the metric, and the structure of `decide()` was unchanged. When the unit is the assay, the test clusters are fewer than 8, so per-assay intervals could not be made and I had to go up to an assay bootstrap.
2. **The negative control is not near 0.5 with a single label permutation, and it is biased depending on the design.** In B① (NT probe), 1 permutation in the first run gave 0.606, and the distribution over 10 permutations was 0.5 ± 0.05. The first design of D③ (shuffle all the labels once and evaluate on the original labels) had a 10-permutation mean of 0.44–0.46, below 0.5, and changing to a design that shuffles only the training labels inside each fold gave 0.50–0.51. The negative control should be reported with several permutations and with only the training labels shuffled, and the expectation should include that the AUC variation of an arbitrary-direction probe is large.
3. **The positive control does not always give 'supported'.** The pretraining effect of A② (8M position probe vs random initialization) had an interval containing 0. If the control is weak, the control should be changed or the reason written.
4. **Be careful at the threshold boundary.** In B①, the same comparison is the same +0.010 when rounded, but it split into 'not supported' and 'supported' depending on the number of layer candidates. The pre-specified minimum effect should be used, but results near the boundary should be read as numbers and not as verdicts.
5. **The directions of the three primary comparisons agreed with modules 03–06, and were not new conclusions.** Simple baselines are strong (position B2, k-mer, ECFP4), and the gain of FMs is small or depends on the setting.

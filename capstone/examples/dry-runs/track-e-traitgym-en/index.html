Track E Dry Run: Reproducing the TraitGym Paper (L1 + L3) — 2026-11-09

English translation. AI-assisted translation of the Korean original. The Korean original is authoritative and may be newer. Links to pages without an English version open the Korean original.

This is a record of running once, following the capstone §3 protocol, TraitGym (Benegas, Eraslan, Song 2025, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.02.11.637758), which was chosen as the 1st-choice candidate in the candidate survey. The scripts are track_E_traitgym_reproduction.py (140 seconds) and track_E_traitgym_claims.py (92 seconds), and they were run on a Mac. For the scores the cache was used, and the public metric CSVs were newly downloaded.

What this record is

  • It is not a learner pilot. The people who made the course ran it once.

  • The paper body was read in step 2 (below, “Comparison with the paper body”). Step 1 (L1, L3) was run before reading, and the “claim unit” mentioned there is one cell of numbers in a public metric CSV. What was read in step 2 is the body text and the appendix text of bioRxiv v2; the figures and tables are images, not text, so they could not be read. In step 3 (“Comparison of tables and figures”), the images of 7 tables and 4 figures were opened and read, and of them the values and orders of Table 1, S4, S8, S9, Figure 5 and supplementary figures S7 and S8 were compared with the cache (since this is reading of images, it may be wrong). 9 tables and 17 figures still could not be read.

  • The reproduction level is only up to the “score → metric computation” of L1. L1 of the protocol is to obtain the numbers with public code and weights, but here, starting from the public score files (the cache), the metric computation was reproduced. The step of running the model to produce the scores was not done. When the NT LLR was computed directly on 210 chr1 variants in module 03, its Spearman correlation with the public scores was 0.87, so the reproduction of that step was not exact.

  • The tolerance and the decision rule were written in the script after reading the public workflow code and before seeing the results. It is not a real preregistration.

  • It received Codex and agy cross-review (see the “Cross-review” section at the bottom).

What was downloaded (within the approved scope)¶

From revision 1fde19555fe8c0a55b1382bdf4c6f7082209f566 of the HF dataset songlab/TraitGym (MIT) (the same as the snapshot in the cache), 22 metric CSVs, 2,058 B in total, were downloaded: the 17 models in mendelian_traits_matched_9/AUPRC_by_chrom_weighted_average/all/ (those whose scores are in the cache) and 5 files …/{3_prime_UTR_variant, 5_prime_UTR_variant, non_coding_transcript_exon_variant, nonexonic_AND_distal, nonexonic_AND_proximal}/GPN_final_LLR.minus.score.csv. They are stored in .cache/traitgym_published/ (excluded from git), and the sha256 of each file is in the run record (provenance.json). Nothing was installed. The workflow code (common.smk, model.smk, Snakefile) was only read.

What was pre-specified (script header)¶

  • L1: |difference| between the public score and the recomputed value ≤ 0.001 (for all 22 values). The computation is deterministic and has no seed variation (so “measure the variation with the seed” in protocol step 3 does not apply), so the tolerance was set to a value smaller than the public se (0.005–0.10; even the smallest se is at least 5 times the tolerance). The se is not used for the verdict, because the public code uses a polars random sequence and polars is not in this environment, so it was reimplemented in numpy; ±15% (7 times the standard error of about 2% of a 1,000-resample bootstrap) was looked at only as a reference criterion.

  • L3: If the Kendall τ between the public all ranking and a variant ranking is ≥ 0.80, it counts as “ranking held”. Subsets with fewer than 30 positives are not judged because their ranking is noise.

  • The metric definition of the public code (ported): compute average_precision_score per chromosome and take the average weighted by the number of variants of the chromosome. The se is the standard deviation of a 1,000-resample bootstrap that resamples the rows of the chromosome table with replacement. The sign of the score follows plus/minus in the file name.

Failure-detection controls (can the check actually fail?)¶

The decision rule of this track is “the 22 values are within the tolerance”, so instead of the ‘positive and negative controls’ used in the other tracks (can the rule output ‘supported’, and are shuffled labels at chance level), we look at whether the check fails on deliberately wrong inputs and definitions. The confirmation on the side that it can pass is L1 itself.

  • ① Flipping the sign: When the sign of the scores is flipped, all 17 leave the tolerance (minimum |Δ| 0.003). The tolerance (0.001) is narrow enough to catch a difference in definition. However, the minimum |Δ| is only 3 times the tolerance, so the margin is not large.

  • ② Shuffling the scores: When the scores are shuffled within match_group, the mean of the weighted AUPRC is 0.124 (positive fraction 0.100), and all 17 differ from the public values. That it is 0.124 and not 0.100 appears to be because the per-chromosome AP comes out higher than the chance expectation in small samples (not confirmed in this run†).

  • ③ A different definition does not match: The pooled AUPRC differs by up to |Δ| 0.049, and the chromosome equal-weight mean by up to 0.077, and all 17 differ from the public values. This shows that the agreement does not come from chance but from the chromosome mean weighted by the number of variants definition.

L3 result: how well does the ranking hold¶

We look at the Kendall τ between the public all ranking (CADD 0.712 > phyloP-241m 0.696 > GPN-MSA_LLR 0.694 > …, with NT |LLR| 0.098 at the bottom) and the variant rankings. The “internal stability τ of a variant” is the mean τ between the rankings obtained by recomputing the same variant with a match_group bootstrap (200 resamples) and the point-estimate ranking of that variant (it is not a null distribution of τ vs public).

  • Reading: The ranking holds even when the metric definition is changed (pooled, equal weight) (τ ≥ 0.94, at the level of internal stability). Of the 5 subsets, 3’UTR was not judged because it has 29 positives, and of the 4 that were judged, 2 (non-coding transcript exon, nonexonic proximal) hold under both metrics. The other 2 (5’UTR, nonexonic distal) do not hold under either metric (the weighted-mean τ of distal is 0.794, near the threshold). The rank-1 model changes to GPN-MSA LLR in distal · pooled and to evo2_7b LLR in 5’UTR · weighted mean.

  • What cannot be said: Although τ (0.69–0.79) is lower than the internal stability τ (0.87–0.90), this is not a test that shows an actual ranking difference of the subsets. The public ranking itself also has sampling variation, and the null distribution of τ(public, subset) (when a subset of the same size is drawn from the whole) was not built. The effect of a few swaps lowering τ because the scores of the lower-ranked models are close to each other (0.098–0.120) was not checked either.

  • Intactness of the match_groups of the subsets: nonexonic_AND_* does not re-fix group completeness after the intersection (the same as the public code), but in this data all 5 subsets had all their match_groups intact, each of size 10 with 1 positive, and there were 0 groups with no positive (29/114/71/34/90). So there was no problem of the group bootstrap drawing broken groups.

  • Only pre-specified variants were looked at. The 5 subsets were chosen because public CSVs exist for them, and the rankings in other subsets, other metrics (AUROC) and another dataset (complex_traits_matched_9) were not looked at.

Comparison with the paper body (step 2)¶

What was read: the main text, the methods, and the appendix A and B text of the full text of bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.02.11.637758 v2 (it is CC BY-NC-ND, so it was not saved in the repository and only summaries are given). curl was blocked by bioRxiv with HTTP 429 (this was not circumvented), and it was read in the built-in browser window. The tables and figures have no content in the page text (they are images), so they could not be read in this step, and some of them were read in step 3 below.

The core claim sentence (protocol step 1 ①, a summary of the abstract and the discussion): Alignment-based models (CADD, GPN-MSA, conservation scores) are advantageous for Mendelian traits and complex disease traits, and functional-genomics-supervised models (Enformer, Borzoi) are better for complex non-disease traits. Evo2 clearly improves with scale but falls short of the alignment-based models, and it is especially weak on enhancers (distal non-exonic).

The metric definition is the same in the paper, the public code and our recomputation: The paper computes AUPRC per chromosome and takes the average weighted by the number of samples (the se of linear probing is a 1,000-resample bootstrap over chromosomes), and it writes that the baseline (random) AUPRC is the positive fraction, 0.1.

Check of the main-text claims (track_E_traitgym_claims.py, 92 seconds, cached scores + public CSVs). The thresholds were written in the script header before seeing the results. The model order of K1 and K2, the 338/3,380 of K5 and the τ of K6 are values that had already been seen in the earlier L1 and L3, so they are not prior predictions. The new bootstrap intervals for K1 and K2 and the new checks for K3, K4 and K7 are the new part of the verdict.

  • For K3, the point estimate is in the same direction as the main text, and it split at the pre-specified threshold. The gain of distal from 1B to 40B (+0.053) is about 1/10 of those of proximal and ncRNA, but the interval [−0.046, +0.145] is wide (34 positives), so it is not established that it is not large. It is uncertain. The same direction is seen from 7B to 40B (+0.033). evo2_1b_base is a base checkpoint with an 8k context, so the gain from 1B to 40B may mix in a difference of checkpoint type in addition to size. The analysis used only the zero-shot LLR we have, and the paper’s stratified result (the Figure 9 caption of the main text) says it uses the better of zero-shot and linear probing. Supplementary figure S8 uses only the Evo2 zero-shot LLR (caption checked in step 3). What was seen in step 3: the bars of that figure agree better with the pooled AUPRC than with the chromosome-weighted mean (an estimate from reading the image), and when K3 is recomputed with the pooled metric, distal is +0.046, inside the threshold, and the verdict changes to ‘supported’. The K3 verdict splits at the boundary depending on the metric (below).

  • The meaning of K5: The paper’s “baseline AUPRC 0.1” is the conventional baseline of the positive fraction. In this data the expected value of a random score for the chromosome-weighted AUPRC is 0.124 (the earlier failure detection ②), higher than 0.1. It seems to be the effect of AP coming out higher than chance level in small samples, but this was not confirmed in this run†.

  • K7 is a point where the paper’s description and the public artifacts seem to disagree, but the conclusion is limited. The public workflow code makes the se of the AUPRC_by_chrom_weighted_average CSV with a 1,000-resample bootstrap that resamples the rows of the chromosome table with replacement (confirmed by reading the code, and in L1 our reimplementation was 0.98–1.07 times the public se). Appendix B.4 of the paper writes that the se of zero-shot is a 100-resample bootstrap within the positive and negative sets. This sentence is ambiguous, so we implemented three interpretations: a chromosome-weighted AUPRC with positives and negatives resampled separately over the whole, a chromosome-weighted AUPRC with positives and negatives resampled separately within each chromosome, and a pooled AUPRC with positives and negatives resampled over the whole. All three implementations give se mostly 0.015–0.03 (0.003–0.008 for models with low AUPRC), which differs by model from the public CSV se (0.005–0.07). The difference seems to be because the chromosome bootstrap of the public CSV captures the heterogeneity of AUPRC across chromosomes (large for strong models and nearly 0 for models near random), whereas variant-level resampling fixes the chromosome composition (not decomposed and confirmed in this run†). Which of the two the error bars in the paper’s figure are could not be confirmed, because the figure could not be read.

  • What could not be checked in step 2: the K1 comparison with SpeciesLM and GPN-Promoter (there are no separate checkpoints in the cache), the complex-trait claims, the linear probing and ensemble claims, and the kinds of zero-shot scores that supplementary tables S8–S9 chose for each model. The K1 comparison with SpeciesLM and GPN-Promoter and S8–S9 were checked in step 3 with the paper’s table values. They were not computed directly from cached scores. The complex traits, linear probing and ensembles were still not checked.

Comparison of tables and figures (step 3)¶

What was read (image reading): From the same bioRxiv v2 page, the lists of 16 tables (Tables 1–5, S1–S11) and 21 figures + Listing 1 (Figures 1–10, supplementary figures S1–S11) were confirmed on the page, and of them the images of 7 tables (Tables 1, 2, 3, S4, S8, S9, S10) and 4 figures (Figures 5, 9, supplementary figures S7, S8) were opened and read. What was compared with the cache is fewer than that. They are Table 1, S4, S8, S9, Figure 5 and supplementary figures S7 and S8, and Tables 2, 3 and S10 and Figure 9 were only read. The images were opened in the built-in browser by following the images pointed to by the “View inline” (tables) and “Open in new tab” (figures) links, and they were not downloaded or saved. The numbers and orders that were read are not kept in the repository because of the paper’s license (CC BY-NC-ND). The script reads from a local values file (.cache/traitgym_paper_values.json, excluded from git), and the comparison results in this document were run with those values. Without the values file, only the recomputation of the public CSVs and the post-hoc analysis run. The figures do not print values, so the bar orders were read, and only for supplementary figure S8 were the bar tips measured from the image pixels (calibrated with the x-axis ticks, at the ±0.01 level). This measurement cannot be reproduced from the repository, and it is an unblinded measurement made after seeing the cache values. The bar-tip pixels and the calibration constants were recorded in the same values file. The bioRxiv page was not downloaded with curl (HTTP 429 in step 2). As an alternative, public APIs were queried. Europe PMC does not have the full text of this preprint (inEPMC = N) and no journal version was found, and the bioRxiv details API returned a non-JSON response and could not be used (no workaround was attempted). The table “View popup” pages were empty (lazy loading by JS), so “View inline” was expanded with an actual click to open the table images.

Checks (track_E_traitgym_tables.py, 5.5 seconds, cached public metric CSVs + score feature files). The rules were written in the script header after reading and before seeing the comparison results. Counts must be exactly equal, AUPRC must differ from the paper value (third decimal place) by 0.0005 or less (the implementation has a +1e-6 floating-point margin), and the number of mismatched pairs in a ranking must be 0 (ties are also counted as mismatches). The checks are counted separately in four kinds: paper↔cache, recomputation of the public CSV, internal consistency of the paper (it compares only transcribed values among themselves, so it cannot independently catch transcription errors), and post-hoc analysis.

Post-hoc analysis (identification of the metric of the figure — added after seeing the results):

  • The bars of supplementary figure S8 agree better with the pooled AUPRC than with the chromosome-weighted mean. The mean |difference| from the 18 values obtained by measuring the bar tips in pixels and converting to AUPRC was 0.003 (maximum 0.007) for the pooled AUPRC and 0.044 (maximum 0.120) for the chromosome-weighted mean (e.g., CADD in distal is 0.723 pooled and 0.835 weighted mean; Evo2 1B in distal and ncRNA, whose bars are shorter than 4 px, could not be measured). This is estimated from reading the image, not a definition confirmed from the caption or the paper body (the limitation above; the “less than 0.01” criterion is also a post-hoc criterion set to match the measurement precision). With the pooled metric, the bar orders of all 5 panels are correct (the 5′UTR mismatch above is explained by the difference of metrics: in the pooled metric CADD 0.575 > Evo2 7B 0.568).

  • If K3 is recomputed with the pooled metric, the verdict changes (the thresholds 0.10/0.10/0.05 are unchanged, and the bootstrap is over the match_groups within the subset): proximal +0.472 [+0.386, +0.548], ncRNA +0.536 [+0.429, +0.645], distal +0.046 [−0.015, +0.137]. Since distal ≤ 0.05, it is ‘supported’ (with the weighted-mean metric it is +0.053, ‘not supported’). The distal values of both metrics are within 0.01 of the threshold and the intervals are wide, so it cannot be said that “it is not large in distal” has been established. The pre-specified verdict for K3 (weighted mean) is left as it is, and this result is read as grounds that the verdict depends on the choice of metric and on the threshold boundary. Why the paper used the pooled metric for this figure, and whether the main text states that fact, could not be confirmed.

  • The gap in K1 was filled with values from the paper’s tables (not computed from cached scores): The best score per model in the paper’s S8 (Mendelian) is lower than Evo2 40B for both SpeciesLM and GPN-Promoter, and the best of the non-alignment-based gLMs is Evo2 40B. Also, S9 shows that the paper’s selected scores for NT, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, Evo2 1B and others are not LLR (L2 distance, inner product), and differ from the LLR scores that K1 used. This does not affect the ranking conclusion of K1 (the selected scores in S8 are all at or below the Evo2 40B LLR).

What this step tells us: The public metric CSVs and feature files are not inconsistent with the paper’s tables (Table 1, 10 cells of S8, S4) and figures (the transcribed orders of Figure 5 and S7). It is limited to Mendelian zero-shot, and the figures are at the level of the order of 11 models. What it does not tell us: how the scores were produced from the model (model-level reproduction), the tables and figures for complex traits, linear probing and ensembles, and the tables and figures that were not read (Tables 4, 5, S1–S3, S5–S7, S11, Figures 1–4, 6–8, 10, supplementary figures S1–S6, S9–S11; all of them per the inventory above).

What cannot be said¶

  • All the claims of the paper. Only the claims related to Mendelian zero-shot in the main text were checked. Complex traits, linear probing, ensembles, and the interpretation of the CADD and Borzoi features were not checked. Whether the public CSVs are the same as the paper’s tables and figures was confirmed in step 3 only for part of the Mendelian zero-shot (Table 1, S4, S8, S9, Figure 5, supplementary figures S7 and S8) (the figures at the level of order, by image reading). The repository has separate mendelian_traits_v21_ and v22_matched_9 folders.

  • Model-level reproduction. It was only shown that recomputing the metrics from the public score files matches the public metrics (this does not mean that the scores themselves are right). How the scores were produced from the model was not reproduced.

  • Other models and datasets. The remaining 64 models of all (the score files are not in the cache), complex_traits_* (about 4.2 GB) and no_cadd_overlap (needs the CADD training-overlap files) were not covered.

What was changed from the template (adjustments needed for track E)¶

  • make_split, fit_predict, seeds and PRED are not needed, because there is no training and the computation is deterministic. The unit is the model (17) and the chromosome instead of the seed.

  • Instead of decide() (effect size, number of wins, interval), the decision rules are the tolerance rule (|Δ| ≤ ε, all 22) and the Kendall τ rule. “Run the original code with only the seed changed to measure the variation” in protocol step 3 does not apply to a deterministic computation, so the tolerance was set from the public se and the rounding level.

  • The meaning of controls changes: failure-detection controls that look at “can the check fail” (flipping the sign, shuffling scores, a different definition) replace the positive and negative controls.

  • The deviation log and the notation of the reproduction level (here “L1: score → metric”) must be put into §1 and §5 of the report.

What was not done¶

  • The tables and figures that were not read (Tables 4, 5, S1–S3, S5–S7, S11, Figures 1–4, 6–8, 10, supplementary figures S1–S6, S9–S11), reproduction of the score-generation step (running the models), the null distribution of the ranking differences of subsets, and the complex_traits and AUROC variants were not done.

  • A learner pilot.

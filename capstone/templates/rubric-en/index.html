Capstone Evaluation Rubric

English translation. AI-assisted translation of the Korean original. The Korean original is authoritative and may be newer. Links to pages without an English version open the Korean original.

Score each of the 8 dimensions from 0 to 3, for a maximum of 24 points. Write down the evidence (file, cell, table) and then assign the score. Get both a self-assessment and an independent review (by a human or a model), and discuss the dimensions where the two differ a lot. The pass and expert criteria below are arbitrary criteria proposed by this course and not a standard.

ScoreMeaning
0Absent or wrong
1Partial: present, but a key element is missing
2Satisfactory: meets all the items under “2 points” below
3Excellent: the “2 points” items + the “3 points” items

Criteria by dimension¶

#Dimension1 point2 points (satisfactory)3 points (excellent)
1Question and claim scopeThere is a question, but no scope or limitationsA one-sentence question, the maximum scope that can be claimed, and an explicit statement of what cannot be said+ defines in advance a falsifiable decision rule and an effect-size criterion that has practical meaning
2Preregistration and change managementA proposal exists, but the primary comparison and decision rule are vagueSplit, metric, primary comparison and decision rule fixed before the results, with a change log+ distinguishes whether a change came after seeing the results, and marks exploratory analyses separately from pre-specified ones
3Data, provenance, leakageOnly a source is given, with no hash, revision or license evidence, or the leakage check amounts to the name of the splitRecords hashes, revisions and versions; deduplicates; gives evidence that the license was looked up (or states “unverified”); measures leakage per split with numbers+ finds the paths that the split did not block, and checks their impact with stratification or a stricter split
4Baselines and controls1 baseline, or an evaluation with a procedure different from the model’s3 or more, including a baseline that learns no parameters (may use train labels, estimated only on train), a simple feature baseline and random initialization, all with the same procedure and the same split+ controls that separate the selection effect/the contribution of pretraining (random initialization fresh for each seed, untrained dictionaries, etc.) and negative diagnostics (shuffling labels or inputs; a diagnostic, not a proof)
5Evaluation and uncertaintyOnly seed means, or intervals that ignore the correlation unitSeed/split repeats, a paired comparison, and a group bootstrap over the correlation unit (only when there are enough test clusters, stating that it is a conditional interval). When clusters are too few, write why the interval is not computed and judge from per-seed values, consistency and a pre-set effect-size rule; state the metric definition (pooling, ties, gallery); state that seed repeats are re-splits of the same data and not independent repeats+ one-factor-at-a-time sensitivity, setting selection separated from the test set (fixed in advance or nested validation), and a distinction between statistics (difference of medians vs median of differences)
6ReproducibilityA notebook exists, but the environment and seeds are not recordedRuns to the end in a clean environment, provenance recorded, code of the executed copy = submitted code+ generates the number sentences and tables with code and checks that the text matches the output, compares repeated runs, reports time and cost
7Interpretation and limitationsThere is interpretation, but no generalization limits or list of what was not doneDistinguishes “directly supported / hypothesis / cannot be said”, presents negative and unexpected results as they are, and has a list of what was not done+ experiments that directly check alternative explanations, and marking of uncertain statements (†)
8Accountability and communicationMentioned only in partChecks the rights to use and release the data and models before use (if they could not be checked, limits the scope of release and redistribution), a dual-use review and a decision on the scope of release, and a report with a clear structure+ documentation at the level of a model/data card, and verifies the points raised in the independent review and records the grounds for accepting or rejecting them

Automatic caps (red flags)¶

If any of the following is present, the corresponding dimension (in parentheses) gets at most 1 point.

  • A setting (layer, epochs, LR, threshold, keywords, etc.) was chosen by test results and that test result was reported as the primary result (regardless of whether it was described as “fixed in advance”). If there is no separate test set, marking it as an exploratory result lifts the cap. (2, 5)

  • Wrote “no leakage” without measuring leakage after the split. (3)

  • Standardization, PCA, feature selection or regularization selection was fitted on data that included the test set. (3, 5)

  • The numbers in the text differ from the run output. (6, 7)

  • Left out negative or unexpected results, or turned them into a generalizing sentence (“FMs are …”). (7)

  • Accepted review points without verifying them, or ignored them without grounds. (8)

  • Used a revision that was not pinned, remote code that was not read, or weights/pickles whose source was not checked. (3, 8)

  • Agreed on the user’s behalf to the terms of a gated model or dataset. (8)

The proposed criteria are arbitrary and are as follows. A pass is a total of 16 or more, with no 0-point dimension and no red flag. The expert-level checkpoint is a total of 20 or more, with every dimension at 2 or more and no red flag. The rubric looks at the quality of the procedure, not at the size or novelty of the result. A negative result can also earn full marks.

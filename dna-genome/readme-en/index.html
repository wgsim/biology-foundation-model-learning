03. DNA · Genome · RNA — DNA Language Models, Variant Effects, Sequence-to-Function

English translation. AI-assisted translation of the Korean original. The Korean original is authoritative and may be newer. Links to pages without an English version open the Korean original.

Duration: Weeks 7–9 (3 weeks, about 24–30 hours) · Compute tier: 🟢 2 local Mac notebooks + 🟢 1 isolated-environment notebook (Enformer) + 🔴 Evo 2 (not recommended, see below) Prerequisites: module 01 (tokenization, frame shift, pitfalls of evaluation), module 02 (zero-shot scores, Spearman, bootstrap).

Notation: † marks a statement that must be checked directly in the paper text or the model card. The cited papers are in sections 03 and 05 of the reading_list. This is information as of 2026-11, and new models appear quickly, so check the model card before you use one.

Learning goals¶

  1. Compare the tokenization, context length and objective (MLM vs autoregressive) of DNA language models (DNABERT, NT, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, GPN, the Evo family).

  2. Explain that supervised sequence-to-function models (DeepSEA, Enformer, Borzoi, AlphaGenome) and self-supervised DNA LMs have different training objectives, and do not put them in the same ranking table.

  3. Build a linear probe on NT embeddings and compare it fairly with a k-mer baseline.

  4. Compute log-likelihood ratio (LLR) variant scores of a DNA LM, evaluate them with TraitGym, and interpret them by comparing them with conservation-based scores (phyloP, CADD).

  5. Summarize the limitations that the critical evaluations (BEND, Huang 2023, TraitGym) point out.

  6. Outline the position and limits of RNA models (RNA-FM, RiNALMo, Ribonanza).

1. Characteristics of DNA data — how it differs from proteins¶

  • Mostly non-coding. The signals that determine function are scattered over short regulatory motifs, spacings and distances.

  • There are long-range interactions. An enhancer can regulate a gene tens to hundreds of kb away, so the context length matters (the attention cost L² of module 01 2.1).

  • You must handle double strands and directionality. Reading from either strand gives an alternative representation of the same molecule, so reverse-complement is handled by augmentation, averaging or an equivariant architecture. However, direction matters in strand-specific tasks.

  • Labels are scarce. It is hard to obtain the “ground truth” of a variant effect experimentally, so evolutionary conservation or experimental tracks are used as proxy signals instead.

  • There are many repeated and duplicated sequences. The same sequence is in many places of the genome, so train/test leakage arises easily (module 01 5.2).

Be careful in interpreting. Enformer-type models are trained with supervision to directly predict experimental tracks, and DNA LMs are trained self-supervised on the sequence distribution alone. They can be compared side by side on the same variant set with the same metric (notebook 02). However, because their training objectives and data differ, you must not claim a general superiority from scale or ranking alone, or apply the weakness of one to the other.

3. Tokenization and context — a review of module 01¶

  • NT v1 cuts the sequence into non-overlapping 6-mers, and handles the trailing part that is not divisible by 6 as single nucleotides. So the frame-shift sensitivity seen in module 01 notebook 01 applies as it is. Changing the start position by only 1 changes all the tokens.

  • Because the tokens do not overlap, if the variant is inside a complete 6-mer, one SNV changes exactly one 6-mer token. So the LLR of NT can be defined as the difference in the log probability of the alt 6-mer and the ref 6-mer when one token is masked. The leftover part at the end of the sequence is made of single-base tokens, so the token that changes is a 1-mer, not a 6-mer. Putting the variant at the center of the window avoids this case.

  • The position embeddings are absolute (per the NT v1 setting) and the training length is 1,000 tokens, so extrapolation to sequences longer than the training length is not guaranteed.

4. Embeddings + linear probe: promoter prediction¶

Hands-on 01_nt_embeddings_promoter_probe

  • The task is to classify whether a 300 bp sequence is a promoter. It uses the NT downstream tasks promoter_tata and promoter_no_tata, and the data is the revised version published on HF.

  • Compare 6-mer frequency + logistic regression with NT embeddings + logistic regression. The former is the simplest baseline, using only the frequencies of the token unit that NT uses.

  • Check the split. This data is divided by chromosome, so test is chr20 and chr21 and train is the rest (check it directly in notebook 01). Duplication in repeat regions could not be confirmed†. A high score can be a signal that the task is easy. With 6-mer frequencies alone, the AUROC is already around 0.93.

  • Pretraining and evaluation overlap. NT v1, trained on the human reference, was pretrained on the whole of GRCh38, so the sequences of the test chromosomes have also already been seen, without labels. The labels of the probe have not leaked, but the 6-mer baseline has no such exposure, so the comparison is not perfectly symmetric (module 01 5.2).

5. Variant effects: zero-shot LLR and TraitGym¶

5.1 LLR definition¶

For the reference allele (ref) and the alternative allele (alt),

LLR=log⁡p(alt∣context)−log⁡p(ref∣context)\mathrm{LLR}=\log p(\text{alt}\mid \text{context})-\log p(\text{ref}\mid \text{context})LLR=logp(alt∣context)−logp(ref∣context)
(1)

It scores how much less plausible the variant is under the “natural sequence distribution” according to the DNA LM. Both the signed LLR and the absolute value absLLR are used. A deleterious variant may have a negative LLR because ref has a higher probability than alt, or it may be “unusual” regardless of direction. That is why TraitGym evaluates both variants.

5.2 TraitGym¶

  • TraitGym is a benchmark made of causal candidates (Mendelian and complex traits) of human non-coding regulatory variants and matched controls. The problem is binary classification and the main metric is AUPRC. In the matched_9 version, with positive : control = 1 : 9, the AUPRC of a random prediction is approximately the positive fraction (about 0.1). In finite samples (especially in a subset with 21 positives) it can differ slightly from this value, so notebook 02 also compares against a null distribution made by shuffling each score’s own values within the match_group.

  • What the authors report in the abstract is as follows. Alignment-based models such as CADD and GPN-MSA are advantageous for Mendelian traits, and Enformer and Borzoi, the functional-genomics-supervised models, are better for complex non-disease traits. Evo 2 improves as the scale grows, but falls somewhat short of alignment-based models, and it is especially weak on enhancer variants. We check this claim directly on some variant sets in notebook 02.

  • The controls are matched. The controls are matched to the positives by variant type, TSS distance and so on, so the information that a simple feature such as TSS distance gives should be small. How much residual predictive power there is depends on the matching method and the sample, so we measure it directly in notebook 02 (it was about 0.115, close to the random 0.10 but not exactly the same).

  • Conservation-based scores such as phyloP and phastCons, and CADD, are strong baselines even without training. Whether DNA LMs exceed these baselines is the key question.

Hands-on 02_zero_shot_variant_scores_traitgym: First evaluate the precomputed per-model scores that TraitGym published. Then directly compute the LLR of chr1 variants with NT v1 500M, trained on the human reference, and compare on the same variants.

6. Sequence-to-function: Enformer¶

  • Enformer takes 196,608 bp as input and predicts 5,313 human tracks, averaged in 128 bp units, for the central 896 bins. It shortens the length with convolutions and integrates long-range interactions with a transformer. The input range and the output range are different. A variant within the input can affect the output, but the tracks at positions outside the output interval are not directly predicted.

  • In silico mutagenesis (ISM) looks at the variant effect by changing one base of the sequence and seeing how much the prediction changes. Scores such as Enformer_L2 in TraitGym are values summarizing such ref/alt prediction differences†.

  • There are limits too. Huang et al. (2023) reported that current models do not explain inter-individual expression variation well and often get the direction of the variant effect wrong. Getting the between-gene differences of the reference genome right and getting the personal cis-variant effect right are different tasks.

Hands-on 03_enformer_tracks (isolated environment .venv-enformer): Predict tracks from the sequence around a gene’s TSS, and check whether the results agree with known biology.

7. Lessons from critical evaluations¶

StudyKey message (as reported by the authors)
BENDThe embeddings of current DNA LMs come close to expert methods on some tasks but capture long-range features only to a limited extent

TraitGymAlignment/conservation-based models are strong baselines, and self-supervised LMs improve with scale but there are still areas where they have not caught up
GPN-MSA (as reported by the authors)It states that earlier human DNA LMs (NT, etc.) were worse than a simple conservation score at predicting variant effects

How to read: These results are about specific models, tasks and datasets. Record them as “in this setting it did not exceed this baseline”, not as “DNA LMs are useless” (the reading principle of module 00).

8. RNA models overview (no notebook)¶

  • RNA-FM is a model trained by self-supervised learning on about 23 million non-coding RNA sequences of RNAcentral, and is used for structure and function prediction.

  • RiNALMo is an RNA language model with 650 million parameters, trained on about 36 million ncRNAs. The authors put generalization of structure prediction to RNA families not seen before as the core claim.

  • Ribonanza and RibonanzaNet are crowdsourced data of the scale of 2 million sequences measured by chemical mapping, and a model trained on it.

  • A common caution: RNA structure prediction has difficulty generalizing across families, so the family-level split decides the results (module 01 5.2).

9. Comprehension check¶

Q1. In NT, which uses a non-overlapping 6-mer tokenizer, how many tokens does one SNV change? Compare with DNABERT, which uses overlapping 6-mers.

Answer
Because the tokens do not overlap, if the variant is inside a complete 6-mer, only **one** 6-mer, the one the SNV belongs to, changes. In overlapping 6-mers, all 6 windows that contain that base change. At a sequence boundary, fewer than 6 change. So the LLR of NT can be defined as the difference in the log probability of one token. The leftover part at the end of the sequence is made of single-base tokens, so it is an exception.

Q2. Why can the NT score differ for the same SNV when the start position of the window changes, and what mitigates it?

Answer
When the frame of the non-overlapping 6-mers changes, the variant lands at a different position inside a 6-mer and all the context tokens also change (the frame-shift sensitivity of module 01). Computing all the start offsets 0–5 and averaging can reduce the frame dependence. However, if you cut the sequence only from the front, the length stops being a multiple of 6, and the effect of the end being split into single-base tokens comes along with it. To see the pure frame effect, make the length a multiple of 6 (the 294 bp control in section 6 of notebook 01).

Q3. What is the difference between LLR and absLLR, and why evaluate both?

Answer
LLR is a signed score (negative if alt is less plausible than ref), and absLLR is the magnitude regardless of direction. For some variants, "how far it departs from the natural distribution" matters regardless of direction, so absLLR may fit better. Which one fits depends on the model and the task, so both are evaluated.

Q4. Why should TraitGym’s TSS distance alone hardly separate positives from controls?

Answer
Because the controls were built to be matched to the positives by variant type, TSS distance and so on. Such matching is designed so that simple confounders cannot inflate the score. If a high score comes from the matching variables alone, suspect the data composition or a code error. However, there is no guarantee that the information is exactly 0. In notebook 02, the AUPRC using only TSS distance was 0.115, close to the random level of 0.10 but not exactly the same. The cause may be the matching method or the sample, so do not settle it, but record it as an observation.

Q5. What is the expected AUPRC of a random prediction, and how does it differ from AUROC?

Answer
The AUPRC of a random prediction is approximately equal to the positive fraction. With 1:9 matching it is about 0.1, and in finite samples it can differ slightly. In contrast, the random expected value of AUROC is 0.5 and does not depend on the class ratio. When positives are rare, AUPRC reflects more sensitively the ability to put positives at the top.

Q6. What can it mean if, in the NT promoter classification, the 6-mer frequency baseline is similar to the embedding probe?

Answer
There are the following possibilities. The task is easy enough to be solved by sequence composition at the 6-mer level alone, such as differences in GC or motif composition, or the embedding cannot add more information than that linearly. Also check the possibility of leakage in the data split, the probe capacity and the layer choice.

Q7. What should you observe when comparing a supervised sequence-to-function model and a self-supervised DNA LM in the same table?

Answer
One is trained on experimental tracks and the other on sequence alone, and their objectives also differ. So do not conclude superiority from scale or ranking. Evaluate on the same variant set, the same metric and the same split, and report the conservation-based baselines together. Also look stratified by which variant types it is strong or weak on.

Q8. Why is the Enformer output shorter than the input (center 896 bins ≈ 114,688 bp)?

Answer
Sequence at the edges lacks context on both sides and is hard to trust, so only the central region is made the prediction target†. Distinguish the input range of 196,608 bp from the output range of the central 114,688 bp. A variant outside the output interval can also affect the central output through the receptive field, but the track at that position itself is not directly predicted. When evaluating variant effects, use a window that places the variant inside the output interval so that the output bins around the variant can be read.

Q9. Why does the result of Huang et al. (2023) not mean that “Enformer is useless”?

Answer
This study evaluated a specific task: **expression differences due to inter-individual cis variants**. Performance on other tasks — expression patterns across genes and cell types, regulatory-element annotation, variant prioritization — is a separate matter. However, read it as a warning that you must be careful when interpreting model scores as individual-level effects.

Q10. If you use published precomputed scores instead of running Evo 2 yourself, what is not guaranteed?

Answer
You cannot control the details of the score computation, such as the window length, anchoring, reverse-complement handling and model variant. The published scores are values computed on the same variant list as the benchmark, but you cannot reproduce and vary every choice of the method in experiments.
